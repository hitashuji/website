Every ridge, slope, and stone wall in Himachal has something to teach a building. Our work is an ongoing conversation with that terrain — sustainable, climate-responsive, and unapologetically local in material and craft.

Architecture

The studio

A small practice for architecture and interior design.

We work in a small team with a small number of clients each year. Most of our work is residential — private houses, apartments, the occasional chalet — alongside a small portfolio of hospitality and cultural projects. We believe architecture is at its best when it is locally rooted, patiently detailed, and quietly lived in.

Dettagli di stile

Every joint, every material, every proportion is a decision.

Our work begins where most projects end — in the way a stone is laid against the slope, the way light enters a deep eave, the joint between timber and concrete. We treat detail as the primary language of architecture, and the mountains as the brief.

Philosophy

The mountains are not a backdrop. They're a collaborator.

Founded in 2017, Hitanshu Jishtu Architects is a small practice in Shimla, Himachal Pradesh. Our work spans residences, residential renovations, cottages, guesthouses, and hotels, alongside urban design and planning — all rooted in a deep respect for the Himalayan landscape. We design slowly, with a small number of clients each year, and we build to last for decades.

  • 01Terrain-first planning. We rarely flatten a site. Buildings step, stagger, and terrace with the slope instead of against it.
  • 02Local material, honest construction. Stone, timber, and slate worked the way local craft traditions intend.
  • 03Climate as design brief. Orientation, insulation, and passive solar gain are decided before aesthetics.
  • 04Restraint over spectacle. Every large window, terrace, and material choice earns its place by serving how the building is actually lived in.

About the studio

From concept
to emotion.

Trained in Switzerland and shaped by practice in London, Bangalore, and Auroville, Hitanshu returned to Himachal to build a practice rooted specifically in the Himalayas — bringing a global architectural training to bear on a very local, very particular set of problems: altitude, seismic risk, snow load, and a landscape that doesn't forgive careless design.
