Daafi House

Theog, Himachal Pradesh · —

Brief

Perched on a forested ridge above Theog, Daafi House doesn't sit on the mountain so much as grow out of it. Stone walls, quarried and stacked in the local vernacular, rise into timber-framed volumes that step down the slope in a loose, deliberate cascade — each mass finding its own angle to catch the light, the view, the wind.

About the project

This is architecture that refuses to flatten the land it's built on. Rather than carving a single pad into the hillside, the design lets the terrain dictate the plan: a cluster of interlinked structures, staggered in height, connected by stone stairways and timber walkways that thread through the site like a village path. From a distance, at dusk, the effect is almost geological — lit windows scattered across the ridge like something that has always been there.

Material honesty. Local stone, deodar wood, and slate-toned roofing carry the material language of the Himachal hills, but the detailing is contemporary — clean joinery, oversized glazing, steel railings that don't compete with the masonry. Nothing is dressed up to look rustic; it simply is rustic, built the way the region has always built, refined for how people live now.

Interiors that hold the warmth in. Inside, exposed timber rafters and rough-plastered walls frame rooms that feel carved rather than constructed — deep window seats, low beamed ceilings, stone accent walls left raw against soft furnishings. Bathrooms in reclaimed wood and stone continue the material story right down to the tap fittings. Every room opens to a balcony or a framed view, because in a house built for the mountains, the landscape is never optional set-dressing — it's the point.

Spaces that gather. A dining hall wrapped in slate and timber, hung with brass bells and folk textiles, turns a meal into a small ceremony. A fire pit terrace looks straight out over the valley for evenings that don't need much else. A quiet reading nook, book-lined in raw pine, gives the house a slower register — proof that Daafi was designed not just to be photographed, but to be lived in.

Daafi House is what happens when a mountain site is treated as a collaborator rather than an obstacle — architecture in dialogue with Theog's terrain, climate, and craft tradition, built to age the way stone and timber age: better.
